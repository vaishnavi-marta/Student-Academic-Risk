Student Academic Performance and Learning Risk

1. Introduction
In this project, I analysed the supplied records of 200 students to explore which study and behaviour measures accompany the three academic risk labels. Students have different study routines, attendance, sleep habits, and assessment results. I used charts and summary tables to study the data. I also used K-Means to group students with similar recorded characteristics and a decision tree to predict the risk category.
The data show large differences in attendance, assignment scores and weekly study time. A three-cluster K-Means model recovered groups that mostly aligned with the risk labels, while a small decision tree correctly classified 34 of 40 held-out records. These results describe this dataset; they do not show that changing a single habit would cause a change in academic risk.

3. Objectives
To understand the dataset and check it for missing or repeated records.
To compare study habits and marks across the three risk categories.
Understand how study habits and attendance relate to academic learning risk. 
To use K-Means to group students with similar characteristics.
Apply clustering and classification algorithms to student performance data.
Interpret the factors associated with Low, Medium, and High AcademicRisk.
To train and evaluate a decision-tree model.
To understand how Gini impurity and entropy help compare possible splits.
4. Dataset overview
The dataset contains 200 students and the following information:
- Fields
Age and gender
Study habits
Study hours per week and attendance percentage
Academic results
Assignment score and previous exam score
Daily habits
Sleep hours and internet-use hours
Outcome
Academic risk: Low, Medium, or High
